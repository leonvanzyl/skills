# The packaging strategy (`strategy.md` in the work folder)

One page that every designer and checker reads. It is written before any image exists, because the images inherit
its mistakes. Write it yourself; do not delegate it.

## 1. The video, honestly
Read the whole transcript. Then write:
- **The hook, verbatim, with times:** everything said in the first 30 to 75 seconds. The thumbnail and title make a
  promise; these lines are what keeps it.
- **The honesty line:** what the video really delivers, with timestamps, and what it does NOT back (no "free" if it is
  a trial, no "full guide" for a ten-minute demo, no "new" unless the thing is new, no number the video does not
  contain). Include the sponsor's rules if there is one.
- **What kind of thing it is:** a tool, a release, a how-to, an opinion, an experiment.

## 2. The viewer and the idea
- **Who clicks:** one or two segments of the channel's audience, described by their situation rather than by this
  video's problem. This is for your own thinking. The profiles the blind read test uses are NOT yours to write: have
  a separate agent that has not read the transcript write them now, before any concept exists (`checks.md`, section
  0), and do not edit what it writes. Profiles written by the strategist carried the video's pain into the viewers and
  rigged the test (viewers written with the video's pain tapped 89%, viewers written to be bored by it tapped 0%).
- **What they already want or fear** that this video touches.
- **The idea in one sentence**, in their words, with the change of state in it ("Claude keeps 'fixing' a bug it can't
  see; give it eyes on the traffic and it finds the real cause").
- **Where the demand is:** check search volume for the subject and for the things the audience already follows
  (`vidiq_keyword_research`). If the video's own subject has no pull (a sponsor's unknown product), make the thing the
  audience follows the subject and the product the answer.

## 3. Thirty ideas, then ten (or the number he asked for)
Write about thirty packages in words only: for each, a click reason, the few words on the thumbnail, the one
picture, and a title. Vary the reason to click, not the colours: recognition of a pain, a change of state, a verdict,
a specific curiosity, a number, an experiment, the plain "what you get", an identity, a warning. Vary the composition
too, in words, before anything is rendered: where he stands, how big, what the object is.
Then cull by asking of each:
- Is it confirmed by the hook? Find the line and the time. If the opening never mentions it, it is clickbait however
  true it is (a "1 MISSING WORD" concept was paid off at 7:22 and never mentioned in the hook; it had to be dropped
  from contention).
- Does every word survive the honesty line?
- Could a stranger say what the video is about from the thumbnail alone, in one second, at 168 px wide?
- Do the thumbnail's words do a different job from the title's?
- Does it need knowledge only part of the audience has (a status code, a header)? Say which concepts do, so the page
  can tell him who each one is for.
- Can anything on it be misread?
Skipping this step is the difference between "ten ideas" and "the first ten ideas".

## 4. Titles
Six to eight, each tied to a concept or usable with several. Under about 60 characters. Check each against the honesty
line word by word: superlatives ("biggest"), news words ("just", "new", "now", "finally") and counts ("3 bugs") are
where overclaims hide. A news word is honest only if the video says the thing is new: on the first run "Finally" was
offered for a product that the video itself describes as having been around for over twenty years. Every title
goes through the promise check, and again if it is reworded. Write a sponsor-named version where a sponsor needs it.
The three packages for the test each need their own title (a combined test compares packages).

## 5. The table
End `strategy.md` with the ten concepts: number, click reason, the exact words, the picture in one line, the best
title pairing, the hook line that confirms it (with its time), and who it is for. Designers work from their row.

## What not to put in it
- Format claims presented as proof. "This format wins" has to survive the control study (`research.md`) before it is
  more than a guess; most did not.
- Rules from the channel report, from a ranking of his own videos, or from earlier runs. What an earlier run found is
  listed in `research.md` as dated hypotheses: things to test again, not things to assume.
