# The likeness pass

Every finished thumbnail goes back through the image model once more, to correct Leon's face and nothing else. This is
his own instruction ("it's just a matter of sending the reference image of myself to Codex, along with the generated
thumbnail, and asking it to correct the likeness").

What it did on the first run (18 images): every corrected face was closer to its portrait than the face it replaced,
and nothing outside him moved (an independent pixel comparison found under 1% of pixels changed outside him). It did
not make them identical. Looked at properly, at native size:
- the smiling faces still had eyes a little more open and a smile a little wider than the portrait;
- the serious faces still had brows tilted up at the inner ends with deeper creases, so they read worried where the
  portrait reads stern, and some had a lower face slightly long or narrow;
- skin had a faint swirl texture at full size (not visible at 1280);
- the pass redrew the logos (see below), and copied pink matting specks from the serious portrait into the hair.
The agents that ran the pass called 15 of 18 "clearly him". That was the agents grading their own work, and it is not
how the result should be described to Leon. His read is the one that counts.

What the independent inspection then found, and what a second round did (same day):
- An inspector that had not seen the pass flagged five of the eighteen faces as likely rejects on expression (a
  frown or glare on serious faces; a grin, wide eyes or raised brows on smiling ones) and three more as at risk.
- Three reads of the same eighteen faces (the orchestrator's, the inspector's, a reviewer's) ranked them differently;
  one read's "least trusted" was another's "closest". Only two images were flagged by all three. Agents cannot rank
  his likeness. Show him reads, not a ranking, and never a verdict.
- The two portraits differ from each other by about 9% in measured face width, so differences under about 10% mean
  nothing.
- Six images went through the pass again with the inspector's notes written into the prompt. The three smiling faces
  came back clearly closer; one serious face improved; two hardly moved in four takes. **The image model keeps most
  of a face it has already drawn.** One more round is worth doing for an expression fault; if a face is still wrong
  after it, re-render the thumbnail from the designer's prompt or swap the concept rather than correcting a
  correction again.
- **The pass re-renders the whole frame, not just his face.** Text survived every time; logos did not (a spark came
  back in the wrong orange). Always re-paste the recorded logos afterwards and look at the result at 3x.

Why it is needed: when the image model renders a whole thumbnail it draws his face from the two portraits and drifts,
the same way every time. Smiling faces come out as a wide grin with too many teeth, raised brows and rosy cheeks.
Serious faces come out as a harder scowl or a worried frown. Both get eyes too wide open, a face too long and narrow,
and a thin moustache. The two prompts name those drifts. If a new drift keeps recurring, add it to the prompt.

Three things this pass must never become:
- **Code on him.** Aligning his portrait to the generated head and blending it in was tried on 2026-11-22. At full
  size it was a soft mask sitting in a head it didn't fit, and he rejected it at once. No paste, blend, warp, scale,
  matte, heal or retouch of his face, hair or body in code, in any form, including pasting a corrected head back onto
  the original image.
- **Its own judge.** The agent that generates the takes may choose between them, but it does not certify the result.
  The final inspection (`checks.md`, section 3) is done by a different agent on the delivered files, and then you
  look, and then Leon decides.
- **A cleaned reference.** Do not fix the portraits by script either (the specks in the hair, the cut-out edge). Tell
  Leon and ask for a cleaner file.

## How to run it
After the designs are final (words fixed, promise check applied and re-applied to anything reworded), list every image
that will be delivered with its full-size source and which portrait it follows (`build/likeness/sources.tsv`: id,
source PNG, `smile` or `serious`). Split the list across three or four agents, each on **Opus 5.5 at extra-high
effort**, and brief each with the text below. The two prompts are in `assets/prompts/` (copied to `{WORK}/briefs/` by
setup): `likeness_smile.txt` and `likeness_serious.txt`. To offer a smiling variant of a serious-faced design (or the
reverse), run the other prompt on the same source: the model changes the expression and corrects the likeness in one
step.

# Likeness pass: bring every face as close to Leon's reference portrait as the image model can

Leon has said that faces generated by the image model can "look a little bit different to the actual avatar image. We
just have to ensure that my likeness is maintained." The model drew his face from two portraits when it rendered each
thumbnail, and it drifts.

His instruction for fixing it, which you follow exactly: send the finished thumbnail and his reference portraits back
to the image model and ask it to correct the likeness. **No code touches him:** never paste, blend, warp, scale,
matte, heal or composite his face, hair or body by script, and never paste a head from one take into another. The
face in the image you deliver is whatever the image model rendered, whole, in one image. Do not write your own
image-processing scripts; the only tool that may alter pixels is `tools.py logo`, on a logo.

Work folder (run everything from here): `{WORK}`   Python: `{PY}`   Skill: `{SKILL}`
Write only inside `build/likeness/` and the `out/` files named for your images.

## For each of your images (`id  source  portrait`, listed in the message that sent you here)
Start by reading his two portraits at full size (`refs/leon/leon_front.jpg`, `refs/leon/leon_serious.png`), so you
know his face before you judge anything. Describe to yourself what you actually see in each (brow angle, lids, mouth
width), and trust that over any description in a prompt.
1. Correct it (two takes; about a minute each; give the Bash call a 600000 ms timeout):
   ```
   {PY} {SKILL}/scripts/gen.py --prompt briefs/likeness_<portrait>.txt --out build/likeness/<id>.png --takes 2 \
     --ref "THUMBNAIL (the image to reproduce, correcting only his face)=<source>" \
     --ref "PORTRAIT of the man, his smile=refs/leon/leon_front.jpg" \
     --ref "PORTRAIT of the same man, serious=refs/leon/leon_serious.png"
   ```
2. Compare at native pixels, never from a shrunk tile. For the source and for each take:
   `{PY} {SKILL}/scripts/tools.py face <image> build/likeness/<id>_<which>_face.jpg --ref <portrait>` puts its face at
   100% beside the matching portrait scaled to the same face height. Read each sheet and compare feature by feature:
   face shape and width, lower-face length, eye shape and openness, lids, brow angle and furrow, nose, mouth and smile
   width, teeth, moustache and goatee, skin, age. Write down what still differs. Is it closer than the source?
3. Check nothing else changed. Read the take whole beside its source: every word letter for letter, every number, the
   layout, the objects, the colours. A take that changed a word, moved things, or added a glow or outline round him is
   rejected.
4. If a take is not close, run once more with a copy of the prompt that names exactly what is still off ("his eyes are
   too wide open", "the inner ends of the brows are raised: make them level as in Image 3") and two more takes. Four
   takes is the limit per image.
5. Pick the closest take. If no take beats the source, keep the source and say so plainly.
6. **Re-paste the logos.** The pass redraws logos. For every logo the designer recorded for this image
   (`out/<concept>.json`, `logos`), paste the official mark again:
   `{PY} {SKILL}/scripts/tools.py logo <chosen.png> <chosen_logo.png> --logo <logo> --box x,y,w,h [--rot deg] --clear`.
   `--clear` removes the mark the model drew first, so the official one goes in at the same size with no fringe of
   the old colour (without it, the only way to hide the old mark is to paste larger, which crowds its neighbours).
   Then Read a 3x crop of the logo and everything within 60 px of it, before and after: the mark sits where the
   model's version was, and no neighbouring letter or shape has been touched. If the image has a logo that was not
   recorded (the model's own drawing on a 3D object), say so in your report: it cannot be pasted.
7. Deliver: `{PY} {SKILL}/scripts/tools.py export <final.png> out/<id>.jpg` (it does not sharpen), copy the final PNG
   to `out/<id>_full.png`, and keep its native face sheet as `build/likeness/<id>_face.jpg`.

## Report
Write `build/likeness/result_<your letter>.json`: a list of
`{"id", "chosen": "take 1 | take 2 | ... | source kept", "closer_than_source": true|false,
"what_was_off_before", "what_still_differs": "feature by feature, plainly; 'nothing' is almost never true",
"logos_repasted": ["..."], "logos_not_pasted": ["..."], "takes_used"}`.
You are not signing these off: another agent inspects the delivered files and Leon judges the likeness. Do not write
"clearly him" or "pass"; write what differs. Reply in six lines at most: one line per image, and which you trust least.

## After the pass
1. Run the final inspection (`checks.md`, section 3) on every delivered file.
2. Look yourself: open every `out/<id>_full.png` whole and its native face sheet. Write one sentence per image on what
   differs from the portrait. That sentence goes on the page beside the image, under "Likeness (your call)".
3. Tell Leon which three you think are closest and which are furthest, and that the call is his.
